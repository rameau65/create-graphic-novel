# Paged direction

## Supported canvas ratios

Lock one generation ratio before page design and keep it consistent through the whole deliverable unless the user explicitly requests a mixed-ratio package.

- **3:4 portrait** — default graphic-novel page and cover. Favor vertical depth, stacked beats, tall establishing shots, and top/bottom negative space.
- **16:9 landscape** — widescreen/cinematic option. Favor lateral staging, horizontal eyelines, panoramic establishing shots, and left/right negative space.

A reference image's dimensions do not silently change the production ratio. Recompose or regenerate for the selected canvas instead of stretching.

## Page rhythm

Select panel density by beat:

| Beat | Typical panels | Direction |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| icon, shock, contemplation | 1–2 | large image, generous silence |
| dialogue, discovery | 3–5 | clear eyelines and reaction beats |
| action, montage, compression | 5–8 | varied sizes, controlled acceleration |

These are ranges, not quotas. Avoid uniform grids unless repetition is the intended effect. For 16:9, reduce unnecessary vertical stacking and use horizontal sequencing when it improves readability; for 3:4, preserve a clear top-to-bottom page path.

## Page-turn logic

- Put anticipation or a question near the end of a recto page.
- Place the reveal after the turn when pagination is known.
- End each page with propulsion, resonance, or deliberate rest.
- Do not split an essential visual sentence across pages unless the turn creates value.

## Panel plan

For every panel record:

`number | size | shape | shot distance | angle | subject/action | emotion | screen direction | continuity | text-safe area`

Use a large panel for the dominant beat, small inserts for details, and borderless or broken panels only when they express memory, rupture, transcendence, or overflow.

## Cover

- Use the same locked ratio as the body unless the user explicitly requests a separate cover ratio.
- One readable focal point and silhouette.
- Clear title zone with sufficient contrast.
- Match the interior's emotional promise and medium.
- Keep essential faces, hands, and symbols away from trim and title areas.
- Avoid dialogue balloons and plot-summary clutter.

## Script placement and protected visual zones

Treat text boxes as an exception, not the default. Prefer placing narration, dialogue, and short script lines directly in intentional negative space when readability remains strong.

- Protect each character's **face, head, torso/body silhouette, hands, and active gesture** as hard no-text zones.
- Protect **important props, symbolic objects, food, tools, vehicles, reflections, environmental clues, and story-critical background objects** as hard no-text zones.
- Add breathing margins around protected zones; text should not merely avoid overlap but also avoid visually crowding them.
- Place script first in naturally empty sky, wall, floor, water, shadow, blank paper, soft-focus background, or other low-information areas that preserve reading order.
- Avoid opaque or translucent rectangular caption boxes whenever direct text can remain readable with spacing, weight, subtle outline/shadow, or a locally calm background.
- If a container is unavoidable, use the smallest shape that comfortably fits the copy, with minimal visual mass and no unnecessary background rectangle.
- Never solve a collision by covering a character or object. Move the text, reshape the composition, or reserve better negative space in the artwork.
- When the script is dense, redistribute text across panels/pages before allowing it to dominate the image.

## Reading and typography

Fix reading order before composition. Reserve outer and binding-safe margins. Place script along the reading path while preserving protected visual zones. Prevent balloon tails from crossing and never cover faces, torsos, hands, decisive actions, or important props. Re-evaluate text-safe zones after changing between 3:4 and 16:9; do not merely scale an old layout.
